MP-01

Reading the reference plan

What the supplied sketch establishes, and what it leaves on the table. The land proportions and the programme are carried forward unchanged; the organising logic is not.

The reference plan is a mirrored, axial composition: a single hotel centred on the beach, two straight ranks of villas flanking it, an inland rectangular basin, and a pair of symmetrical residential fans filling the southern two thirds. It reads clearly at a glance, and that legibility is worth keeping.

Three things in it are structurally sound and are retained. First, the north–south central axis from the highway to the sea — on a site 420 m wide and 1,000 m deep, a single spine is the only way to make the depth comprehensible. Second, hospitality and villas hold the beach, with density pushed inland; that is the correct value gradient. Third, the marina sits mid-site, where it can serve both the beach district and the residential districts rather than belonging to only one of them.

Everything else is a diagram of intent rather than a plan. The basin is a rectangle whose entrance is drawn on its inland side and never reaches open water. The residential fans are a road pattern first and a neighbourhood second — loops drawn for geometry, with plots hung off them. Buildings run up to the boundary on three sides. The strip mall, the second hotel and the gates are named in the legend but not resolved on the ground.

Carried forward

Programme & proportion

420 m × 1,000 m, ~700 units, 70 yachts to 50 ft, 14 villas, 210 m of retail frontage, 3 gates. Nothing is traded away.

Carried forward

Value gradient

Hospitality and signature villas on the sand; density stepping inland; the marina as the middle ground that both halves share.

Replaced

Mirror symmetry

A coastline is not symmetrical and neither is a working harbour. Symmetry here costs frontage and produces two of everything the operator only needs one of.

Replaced

Road-led geometry

The residential fans spend roughly a third of their land on asphalt. Re-cutting the blocks around water and landscape recovers most of it.

MP-02

Weaknesses and the opportunity in each

Twelve issues, ordered by how much value each one costs. Every response is carried into the plan on MP-04.

01 The marina never reaches the seaThe basin is landlocked and its notional entrance points inland. As drawn it is an ornamental lake, not a harbour. ResponseA 45 m dredged approach channel on the western edge, running 340 m from twin training jetties into the basin. The whole beach stays continuous east of it.
02 A rectangular basin wastes its own perimeterStraight quays give the shortest possible water edge for the water area, and no address hierarchy along it. ResponseAn irregular basin with headlands, a plaza bay and an island: 4.10 ha of water generating ~1,180 m of quay, and four distinct water addresses instead of one.
03 Only 420 m of the site can ever see the seaRoughly 4% of the land has natural waterfront. The rest competes on nothing. ResponseManufacture water. Channel, basin and a central lagoon lift activated water edge to ~3,140 m — 7.5× the natural coastline — and put 41% of units on water.
04 Buildings sit on the boundary wallVillas and residential blocks abut the perimeter on the east, west and south. No buffer, no fire access, no edge. ResponseA mandatory 27 m perimeter section on every wall: 12 m planting, 7 m fire and service lane, 8 m setback planting. See the detail on MP-04.
05 Fourteen villas in two straight rowsRank-and-file plots give the rear row a view of the front row's roofs, and give the front row no privacy from the road behind. ResponseA crescent, not a row: three plots on the sand around a signature villa, four on a raised terrace behind, and 10 m green view corridors cut through to the water.
06 One hotel is drawn; two are requiredThe brief needs two 70-key hotels that do not compete for the same guest, the same view or the same arrival. ResponseTwo opposite products 600 m apart: a low horizontal beach resort on the sand, and a compact five-storey marina hotel on the harbour's south-east headland.
07 A single gate for every kind of trafficResidents, guests, retail visitors and refuse trucks would share one throat, and the retail frontage is unresolved. ResponseThree gates 178 m apart along the frontage, each with one job, plus a screened service lane inside Gate 2. The strip mall is split to frame Gate 1.
08 Roads consume the residential districtsThe looping fans are a circulation diagram with plots attached. Frontage is spent on turning geometry. ResponseThe boulevard splits into two 11 m one-way carriageways around the lagoon. Roads and parking fall to 15% of site while walkable frontage rises.
09 No separation of service from guestHotel deliveries, marina fuel and refuse would arrive through the same porte-cochère as the guest. ResponseA continuous 7 m perimeter lane doubling as fire access reaches every back-of-house yard from behind. No service vehicle enters the boulevard.
10 Landscape is infill, not structurePlanting fills the gaps left over after roads and plots are set out, so it can be value-engineered away. ResponseLandscape is set out first as five corridors — dune, sea axis, channel park, lagoon spine, perimeter belt — that the plots are then cut around.
11 Nothing marks arrival from the seaApproaching by water there is no landmark, and no way to find the entrance. ResponseA 22 m harbourmaster tower at the channel head, on axis, visible from 6 NM; the marina hotel behind it as the second, taller marker.
12 No phase can stand aloneWith one gate and no serviced first parcel, nothing can open until almost everything is finished. ResponseFour phases, each independently trading: retail and marine works first, hospitality second, marina hotel third, residential last. See MP-10.
MP-03

Zoning strategy

The site is read as seven bands across its 1,000 m depth, with height inverted against the shoreline and three independent access systems threaded through them.

Bands, north to south

Depth is the site's difficulty and its asset. Banding it makes the walk legible and lets each phase be handed over complete.

0–32 m · Beach and dune. Sand, boardwalk and light pavilions only. No permanent structure within 30 m of mean high water.

32–218 m · Beachfront hospitality. Hotel 1, the Sea Axis Park and the Royal Crescent. Nothing above 13 m.

236–330 m · Marina villas. Seven plots with private berths, addressing the harbour's north quay.

345–565 m · The harbour. Basin, piers, island and quays — the centre of gravity of the whole destination.

556–700 m · Marina Village. Retail, restaurants, yacht club, quay terraces and the marina hotel. The only genuinely urban part of the plan.

650–896 m · Residential districts. Four garden courts, two townhouse precincts and the central lagoon.

900–1,000 m · Arrival and retail. The 210 m strip mall in two wings, the three gates, and everything the public may reach without passing a barrier.

On the seven front villasThe plan is honest about a constraint the brief cannot dissolve: 420 m of coastline, shared with a 115 m hotel and a 35 m public sea axis, cannot give seven royal plots primary beach frontage. Road hierarchy

Main Boulevard, 36 m. Gate 1 to the Arrival Circle: two lanes each way either side of a 12 m planted median carrying a water rill and a double row of date palms. It is deliberately short — 70 m — because a boulevard that runs the whole 1,000 m would be a runway.

Twin carriageways, 11 m each. North of the Arrival Circle the boulevard splits and runs one-way up either side of the Central Lagoon, rejoining at the Marina Circle. Two 11 m streets move the same traffic as one 30 m road, at half the crossing distance, and put water rather than asphalt on the main axis.

Collectors, 15 m. West (residents) and east (VIP, hotels, marina), each running the full depth of the site so that no through-traffic ever needs the boulevard.

Perimeter lane, 7 m. A continuous loop inside the buffer serving every back-of-house yard and doubling as the fire tender route. Hydrants at 90 m; no dead end exceeds 45 m; 12 m turning heads throughout.

Pedestrian priority

Five places where the pedestrian outranks the car, each with vehicles held at its edge: the dune boardwalk (420 m), the Sea Axis Park, the Marina Loop — a 1,050 m promenade around the south, east and west quays — the Marina Plaza, and the lagoon walk.

The north quay is the deliberate exception: it is private and gated at both ends, serving only the seven marina villas and their berths. A public promenade running past seven villa gardens would destroy the product; keeping it private also gives the marina a defined public face on three sides rather than four ambiguous ones.

Longest walkMarina Plaza to the water's edge on the beach: 430 m, entirely shaded — the Sea Mews, the villa quay crossing, and the Sea Axis Park. Marina Plaza to the Strip Mall: 340 m.

Marina — marine engineering

The single most technically demanding element, and the one that carries the project's identity.

Why the channel is on the west edge. A central entrance would cut the 420 m beach in two and leave neither half long enough for a resort. Pushed to the western boundary, the channel takes land that was perimeter buffer anyway, leaves 295 m of unbroken beach east of it, and turns its own east bank into a 340 m promenade — the Marina Walk — linking sand to harbour.

Offset jetties. The two training jetties are unequal (120 m west, 90 m east) and the entrance line is rotated 15° off shore-normal, so the dominant north-westerly swell cannot run straight down the channel. Wave penetration into the basin is held below 0.3 m in the design condition.

Littoral drift is a cost line, not a detail. Any pair of jetties on a drift coast will trap sand updrift and starve the beach downdrift. The plan budgets an annual bypass of 15,000–25,000 m³ and a standing nourishment allowance for the downdrift beach. A monitoring programme should begin at least one full season before dredging so the baseline is real.

Water quality. A single narrow entrance is the classic way to build a stagnant harbour. The basin is therefore given a secondary 6 m culvert at its eastern end with tidal-exchange pumps, targeting complete exchange in under five days. Every stormwater outfall discharges through oil and silt interceptors; none discharges to the beach.

Air draft. The island footbridge and the plaza-to-hotel bridge both cross non-navigable ornamental inlets dredged to −1.2 m. No bridge crosses a fairway, so no lifting span is needed and no operator is required to open one.

MP-08

Landscape and water strategy

Landscape is set out before the plots, as five continuous corridors. Plots are then cut around it, so it cannot be value-engineered away later.

Corridor 01 · 420 m

Dune & boardwalk

Stabilised dune planting — Tamarix, sea daffodil, marram — behind a raised timber boardwalk. No hard structure inside 30 m of mean high water. Shade from cabana clusters, not buildings.

Corridor 02 · 186 m

Sea Axis Park

A 35 m green finger between the hotel and the villas, with a 7 m reflecting rill running its length and terminating in a public beach pavilion. The only free sightline from the marina to the sea.

Corridor 03 · 340 m

Channel park & Marina Walk

Both banks of the approach channel: a wild coastal park to the west, a shaded promenade of washingtonia and date palm to the east, connecting sand to harbour on foot in six minutes.

Corridor 04 · ~860 m edge

Lagoon spine

The central lagoon, its island club and its two townhouse banks. Freshwater, filtered, with a swimming lagoon at the north head and a shaded jogging loop at the water's edge.

Corridor 05 · 2.72 ha

Perimeter belt

A 12 m planted belt inside every wall, thickened to 31 m on the east where the collector runs. Layered ficus, olive and oleander — a green horizon from inside, a solid screen from outside.

Palette

Water discipline

Native and adapted coastal species; turf restricted to the crescent court, the lagoon banks and the four garden courts. Irrigation demand ~900 m³/day, met from treated effluent, not potable supply.

The four-water sequence. Arriving through Gate 1, a visitor crosses water four times in 900 m: the fountain in the Arrival Circle, the Central Lagoon, the harbour basin at the Marina Plaza, and the sea itself at the end of the Sea Axis. Each is a different kind of water — ornamental, recreational, working, wild — and each is visible from the one before it. That sequence, not the boulevard's width, is what makes 1,000 m of depth feel like an arrival rather than a drive.

The irrigation gap is real and should be designed for now. At full occupancy the landscape wants roughly 900 m³/day and the treatment plant will return about 680. The 220 m³/day difference is closed in one of three ways, in order of preference: reduce turf area in the garden courts by a further 15%; add a small polishing RO train to lift effluent recovery; or contract tankered TSE for the summer peak. Assuming it will simply work is how coastal schemes end up irrigating with potable water at four times the cost.

Phasing the plant. The district cooling plant is built in two 2,000 TR trains so that Phase 1 — retail, marine works and the first residential terraces — does not carry the capital cost of capacity it will not use for six years.

Sustainability targetsSea-water-source district cooling condensers using the marina basin; PV on the strip mall and parking canopies (~1.1 MWp); 100% LED with astronomical dimming on all public lighting; a dark-sky lighting regime on the dune to protect turtle nesting; and construction dredge spoil re-used for site levelling rather than exported.

MP-11

The plan, explained

One idea, and the six decisions that follow from it.

The idea. This site has one genuinely scarce asset — 420 m of coastline — and 1,000 m of land behind it that the coastline cannot reach. Every masterplan for a site this shape is really an answer to one question: what do you give the back 800 m? The reference plan's answer was landscape and road geometry. This plan's answer is more water. A sea-fed harbour and a freshwater lagoon convert 420 m of natural shoreline into roughly 3,140 m of activated water edge, and put 41% of the units on it. That single move is what carries the scheme's value, its identity and its walkability.

One: the marina is the plan, not an amenity. It sits at the centre of the depth, so every district is within 400 m of it. It is dredged from the sea, so it is a harbour rather than a lake. Its edges are irregular, so 4.10 ha of water yields ~1,180 m of quay and four separate addresses — private villa quay, hotel quay, village quay, yacht club headland. And it is the terminus of the boulevard, so arrival by road ends at water.

Two: the channel goes to the edge so the beach stays whole. Cutting the entrance through the middle of a 420 m beach would leave two 180 m fragments, neither of which can carry a resort. On the west boundary it costs only buffer land, leaves 295 m of continuous sand, and its east bank becomes a 340 m promenade.

Three: two hotels that cannot be confused. Hotel 1 is horizontal, three storeys, on the sand, entered from a quiet coastal road — a beach resort. Hotel 2 is compact, six storeys, on the harbour's south-east headland, entered from the VIP gate, with a rooftop that looks down the length of the basin — a lifestyle marina hotel with the banqueting and meeting space the resort deliberately does not have. They are 600 m apart, they share no arrival, and they sell to different guests in different seasons.

Four: villa privacy is bought with geometry, not walls. The Royal Crescent turns its back on the road and opens onto a shared court, with two 10 m view corridors cut through to the water and a 1.5 m terrace lifting the second tier. The seven Marina Villas get something the beach cannot offer: a private, gated north quay with a berth at the foot of each garden. Both groups are reached from the east collector without passing a single apartment.

Five: the road network is deliberately thin. Splitting the boulevard into two 11 m one-way carriageways around the lagoon does three things at once — it halves the crossing distance for pedestrians, it puts water instead of asphalt on the main axis, and it recovers roughly 1.2 ha that a single 30 m boulevard would have consumed. Roads and parking together are 15% of the site.

Six: the perimeter is a product, not a leftover. Twenty-seven metres of wall-to-building section on every boundary, with the fire and service lane buried inside it. That lane is why no delivery vehicle ever enters the boulevard, why every back-of-house yard is reached from behind, and why no dwelling is more than 45 m from a fire tender. The rule earns its land three times over.

What the plan gives upIt gives up symmetry, which the reference drawing had and which photographs well. It gives up four beachfront villa plots, because the arithmetic of 420 m does not permit them. And it gives up a straight, uninterrupted 1,000 m vista from gate to sea — the axis is held, but the eye crosses a lagoon, a roundabout and a harbour on the way, which is the more interesting journey and the more valuable one.
MP-12

Recommendations

Where further value can be taken out of this plan, by discipline. Ordered within each group by return on effort.

Commercial value

  • Split the mall's tenancy logic by wing. West wing convenience-led around a 2,200 m² premium grocery serving residents daily; east wing lifestyle- and F&B-led facing the VIP gate. One 210 m run of identical units would compete with the Marina Village and lose.
  • Charge the Marina Village a percentage rent, not a fixed one. Its F&B trade is driven by berth occupancy and hotel keys, both of which the developer controls. Percentage rent aligns the marina operator's incentives with the landlord's.
  • Hold four plaza-front units back from the first leasing round for a destination restaurant. One anchor operator on the plaza is worth more than four average tenants, and cannot be recruited later once the frontage is let.
  • Put the pharmacy, clinic and services on the mall's rear elevation facing the internal parking court, so the highway frontage stays entirely food, retail and terrace.

Residential value

  • Sell the seven Marina Villas with the berth in the title, not as a licence. A deeded 15 m berth is the single highest-margin feature in the entire scheme and is not replicable anywhere else on this coast.
  • Stagger the Garden Court release by court, not by floor, so each court completes with its garden and pool finished. Buyers pay for a finished courtyard; they discount an occupied building site.
  • Give every Lagoon Townhouse a roof terrace. At G+2 with a 9 m ridge, the third level clears the row opposite and converts a garden-view product into a water-view one for almost no cost.
  • Cap Garden Court unit sizes and add a fifth product instead. Duplex garden maisonettes at ground level widen the buyer pool and lift ground-floor values, which are otherwise the weakest in every court.

Hotel experience

  • Give Hotel 1 the beach club as a separate P&L with its own entrance from the boardwalk, so it trades to residents and day guests out of season instead of sitting dark.
  • Put Hotel 2's ballroom at podium level with its own porte-cochère off the east collector. A 400-guest wedding must never arrive through the hotel lobby.
  • Contract the two hotels to different operators or different brands within one group. Two 70-key properties under one flag, 600 m apart, will cannibalise rate rather than build it.
  • Reserve a service tunnel alignment now between the utilities compound and Hotel 2's basement. It is cheap as a reservation and impossible to add once the quay is built.

Marina experience

  • Appoint the marina operator before the dredge design is fixed. Berth mix, pontoon spec, fuel and pump-out positions and the fairway widths all change with the operator's own standards; retrofitting them is an order of magnitude more expensive.
  • Build the harbourmaster tower in Phase 1. It is the cheapest landmark in the scheme, it is what sells the marina from the water, and it makes the harbour look operational while the buildings are still rising.
  • Programme the plaza for twelve months, not four. A winter sailing school, a Saturday market and a regatta series keep the quay alive off-season, which is what persuades the F&B tenants to sign.
  • Reserve six berths for transient visitors. Fully sold-out marinas are dead marinas; transient traffic is what makes the village trade.

Traffic & landscape

  • Model Gate 1 against the mall's Friday evening peak, not an average day. Three lanes in with two holding bays before the barrier, or the queue reaches the highway.
  • Run a shaded electric shuttle on the collector loop from day one. It is what allows the boulevard to stay narrow and the parking to stay peripheral.
  • Plant the perimeter belt and the boulevard in Phase 1, ahead of the buildings. Semi-mature palms need five years; planting them with the last phase means the project is never photographed mature.
  • Commission the beach-monitoring baseline before dredging. Without it, every future erosion claim becomes the developer's to disprove.
